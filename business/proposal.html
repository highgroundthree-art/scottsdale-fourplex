01 · THE NEED

Working families are squeezed out of the middle.

Teachers, tradespeople and young couples earn too much for subsidy and too little to buy in Scottsdale. The homes that fit them, duplexes and fourplexes, were zoned out for decades.

02 · THE CONCEPT

One big house.
Four homes inside it.

From the street it reads as one high-quality house. Inside are four complete homes, each with its own front door, address and utilities.

  • The owner lives in one unit and rents the other three. Rent offsets the mortgage.
  • Built for families living together: grandparents, adult children and young couples under one roof, each with privacy.
  • Not "low-income looking." Real materials, quiet floors, generous light. Homes people are proud to come home to.
  • Separate everything: four water meters, four electric meters, four gas meters, four water heaters, four drains.
  • Condo-ready: rated separations and independent services keep the option to sell units individually later.
03 · WHY NOW

State law made the fourplex legal again.

Arizona HB 2721 took effect January 1, 2026. Scottsdale complied with Ordinance 4690 (adopted Nov. 17, 2025): a fourplex is now allowed on qualifying single-family lots within one mile of Old Town, approved by staff with no rezoning.

04 · BUSINESS MODEL

We sell a business, not just a building.

Each fourplex is sold whole to one owner-occupant who lives in one unit and rents three.

  • Exactly four units on purpose. One to four owner-occupied units qualify for residential mortgages (FHA, VA, conventional). Five or more is commercial lending, so we never add a fifth.
  • Rent helps the buyer qualify. Lenders typically count about 75% of projected rent from the other units.
  • Veterans are a natural buyer pool. VA loans allow 2–4 unit owner-occupied purchases, typically with no down payment.
  • FHA 3–4 unit loans must pass a self-sufficiency test, so rental units are tuned for rent per square foot and the owner unit for long-term living.
Pro forma in progress: purchase price, loan options, payment, market rents and the owner's net monthly housing cost. Financing terms to be verified with a lender before marketing.

06 · BUILT TO LAST

Quality you can't see is where we spend first.

Safe

Wood frame (Type V-B) with residential fire sprinklers (NFPA 13R) and 1-hour fire separations between every unit.

Solid

Post-tension slab foundation, chosen for longevity and lower warranty exposure.

Quiet

Decoupled ceilings under every upper unit, targeting STC/IIC 50. Bedrooms over bedrooms, wet rooms over wet rooms.

Independent

Each unit has its own meters, water heater, drain and cleanout. No plumbing in party walls.

Flexible fuel

One set of prints serves gas or all-electric: tankless gas or heat-pump water heaters.

No field hacks

Open-web floor trusses with planned chases. No cutting joists or beams for ducts or pipes.

07 · DIGNITY BY DESIGN

Every fourplex includes a fully accessible home.

One unit in four (25%) is built to the Type A accessible standard, beyond what code requires for a building this size.

  • Roll-in shower on a recessed slab, not a retrofit
  • Zero-step shared entry (Model 1) and an accessible route from the van-accessible parking space
  • Every bedroom has an escape window, a closet and a smoke alarm
  • Compact 3-bedroom plans keep kids' rooms next to the hall bath
  • Built-in trash and recycling pull-outs in every kitchen
  • Professional landscaping managed through an owners' association

Affordable should never look or feel temporary.

First target: south Scottsdale neighborhoods within one mile of Old Town, often 1950s–60s ranch houses on large lots, many with alleys.

Land reality: south Scottsdale land has been listed above $2 million per acre, roughly $500,000 for a 10,000 SF lot, or about $125,000 of land per unit. Partners that hold land in trust (next sheet) can take that cost out of the price.

Rollout: Scottsdale set first, then City of Phoenix, then Maricopa County, each with its own jurisdiction-specific drawing set.

09 · PARTNERS

Proven local partners for land, buyers and tenants.

Newtown CDC

Community land trust active in Scottsdale. The land stays in a 99-year trust, so buyers pay for the home and not the dirt.

Habitat Central Arizona

Builds attached homes with cities, including 18 townhomes with Tempe. Buyers use sweat equity and a no-interest mortgage.

City of Scottsdale

About $294,000 in HOME funds and $1.06 million in CDBG funds planned for 2026–27.

Veteran housing

VASH vouchers through a veteran nonprofit partner such as Operation Enduring Gratitude can back rents for veteran tenants.

Sources: Newtown CDC; Habitat for Humanity Central Arizona; City of Tempe; City of Scottsdale FY 2026/27 Annual Action Plan. Partnerships are proposed, not yet committed.
